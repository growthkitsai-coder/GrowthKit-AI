Same italic G — but with a techy seam.

Five takes on Option 02. Each one keeps the editorial serif G and the single accent dot, but adds a small, deliberate asymmetry — a notch, a missing pixel, a circuit cut, a terminal glyph — so the mark stops looking like a generic monogram and starts looking like it belongs to a tool, not a tea brand.

·Recommendation

Lead with Option C — Terminal Frame.

It's the one that does the heaviest lifting on "this is a tool, made by engineers, for engineers." The corner brackets read instantly as a developer's selection scope — a marker you've seen in every IDE and CLI you've ever used — and the caret block where the dot used to be ties the wordmark to the editorial accent without losing its tech register. Of all five it's the most distinctive at scale, and the only one that survives at favicon size by simply dropping the brackets.

If C feels too literal, D — Slashed is the same idea at half-volume.
