# Handoff: Avi Aggarwal's LinkedIn Writing Voice

> **Purpose:** Enable any agent to draft LinkedIn posts in Avi's voice without retraining.
> **Built from:** A long working session (June–July 2026) drafting ~15 posts with Avi, plus sample posts he supplied from two peers (Joel and Pranav) whose styles he wants blended into his own.
> **Next session will be used for:** writing more LinkedIn posts in this voice.

---

## 1. Who Avi is (context that shapes the voice)

- Avi Aggarwal, co-founder of GrowthKit AI (AI market-intelligence SaaS), Youth Ambassador for the London Borough of Barnet, secondary school student in London currently sitting end-of-year finals.
- Builds in public. Audience: young builders, student founders, UK startup scene.
- Recent post subjects: Claude Code, the Fable 5 model, the 8x Mobile Hackathon at Entrepreneur First (where he built "Goose" — goose-lockin.vercel.app), balancing exams with building, recruiting interns for GrowthKit.
- His LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/avi-aggarwal-build-ready
- Note: "EF" = **Entrepreneur First** (no apostrophe-s, not "Entrepreneur's First").

## 2. The voice in one paragraph

Reflective and honest, casual but composed. He tells you where he actually is — including when he doesn't have the answer — rather than teaching you a lesson. Humour is dry and self-deprecating, woven in rather than performed. Openers are concrete scenes or outrageous one-liners, never throat-clearing. Closers offer company, not advice. He'd rather sound like a person thinking out loud than a thought leader concluding.

## 3. Two registers (pick one per post)

**A. Reflective register** — for personal essays, founder reflections, anything company-adjacent.
- Proper capitalisation and punctuation (he said: "I'm still representing the company and my own image").
- Casual vocabulary stays: "kinda," "write-off," contractions everywhere.
- Example subjects: exam season vs building, who he was a year ago, the midnight-workflow confession.

**B. Memey register** — for hackathons, product launches, fun/announcement posts.
- All lowercase, looser punctuation, "lol"/"lmao"/"ig"/"rn"/"u" acceptable, occasional emoji (🪿 😭 💀 — sparse, never decorative rows).
- Comma splices are a feature: "it writes the message for you, it is extremely mean."
- Example subjects: the Goose launch post, Fable 5 reaction, going-to-the-hackathon post.

When unsure, ask which register — or infer: is this post *about him/the company* (A) or *about a fun thing that happened* (B)?

## 4. The three source voices being blended

**Avi's own base:**
- "A year ago I... / now I..." reflective structure; holds tension without resolving it ("I don't have the clean answer").
- Adds self-deprecating parentheticals when he edits drafts himself: "(well kinda, I used to write like a monkey so there's no way I'm showing you guys)".
- Casual intensifiers: "wayyy wider," "pretty curious," "wanna," "gonna."
- Ends posts with a parenthetical PS — a photo note or an inside joke: "(By the way, the pic's from a trip to Cornwall...)".

**Joel (peer, style source):**
- Opens on a concrete scene with specific numbers and times: fell asleep on the bus, an hour late, 4am alarms, "2 minutes until registration."
- Self-call-out arc: "I used to think X was proof of discipline. Now I realize I was lying to myself."
- Uses his own analytics as evidence (connection counts, view percentages).
- Confident, punchy final lines. Writes *into* live debates (grind culture, sleep, networking).

**Pranav (peer, style source):**
- Lowercase, line-break heavy, talks directly TO the reader: "you're comparing your day one to someone else's year five."
- Observation → reframe → warm encouragement ("keep going. trust your own pace.").
- Consistency/trust-the-process thesis; self-aware humour ("still waiting for my legs to forgive me").
- Photo PS: "(ps: a really cool pic i took in Dallas at VEX Worlds)".

**The blend formula that emerged:**
1. **Open** like Joel: a specific scene or moment, real numbers/times ("11pm," "before 3pm," "80%"), OR an outrageous hook under ~210 characters (LinkedIn's "see more" fold).
2. **Middle** like Avi: reflective, honest about the tension, no resolution forced.
3. **Turn** like Pranav near the end: a warm line toward the reader that offers company, never instruction — "if you're somewhere in the same mess right now, you're not the only one."
4. **Optional PS** in parentheses: photo note or inside joke (recurring bit: nodding to Pranav and Joel).

## 5. Hard rules (never violate)

- **No em dashes.** Use full stops, commas, or colons instead.
- **No engagement-bait closers.** Never "Agree?", "What do YOU think? 👇", "Repost if...".
- **No "this is for you" lecturing.** Don't tell readers what they HAVE to do or frame the post as being *for* a target group from above.
- **No thesis-y "here's the lesson" framing.** No "Here's what nobody is talking about," no "The lesson?", no numbered takeaways in reflective posts.
- **Avoid the word "tough"** (he explicitly cut it once; prefer "hardcore," "grinding," etc.).
- **Don't resolve what he hasn't resolved.** If the honest position is "I don't know," the post ends there.
- **One callback maximum.** A hook echoed once at the close is fine; a neat loop reads as written.

## 6. Anti-AI-writing checklist (run the humanizer skill, but internalise these)

These specific patterns were repeatedly caught and removed in session:
- **Rule-of-three:** "nags you, guilt-trips you, and roasts you" → collapse to one or two beats.
- **Negative parallelism:** "Not a to-do list. A creature that hunts you." → use at most once ever, prefer plain description.
- **Parallel paragraph structure:** three features each as setup+punchline = AI fingerprint. Make beats uneven: one gets a story, one gets a single sentence, one gets an aside.
- **Engineered punchlines → real anecdotes:** "(\"that is a photo of your ceiling. nice try.\")" became "i accidentally sent a picture of my ceiling at one point and it told me..." — recollection beats structure.
- **Stock "unhinged" vocabulary:** "zero respect for personal space," "has no boundaries" — the model's default reach; find a specific detail instead ("has opinions about your screen time").
- **Add recall-shaped specifics:** times ("before 3pm"), percentages ("both at about 80%"), named places.

> (By the way, the pic's from a trip to Cornwall that I went on a year ago)

## 8. Workflow notes for the next agent

- **Hooks matter most.** He frequently asks for "a better hook" — offer 3–8 options across styles (scene-based, confession, reframe, question) with a recommendation. Outrageous is welcome; lording over readers is not.
- **He iterates.** Expect 3–6 revision rounds per post. Common asks: "more casual," "more reflective," "less about me / more about others" (and sometimes the reverse), "remove [specific word/line]."
- **Placeholders:** when people need tagging, insert `[name]` and tell him to tag them in LinkedIn.
- **Link placement:** flag the link-in-comments-vs-body tradeoff for reach when a post contains an external URL.
- **Facts:** search before referencing news/products; he posts about fast-moving AI news and accuracy has mattered (e.g. correcting event names, offer details).
- **Standing joke:** if he asks to be roasted in one word, the answer is always "Performative."
- A condensed version of these rules also lives in the assistant's persistent memory; this file is the full spec.
